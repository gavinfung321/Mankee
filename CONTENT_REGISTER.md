## Proposed language policy

- Traditional Chinese for Hong Kong (`zh-HK`) is the source and primary interface language.
- Copy should use natural Hong Kong vocabulary and tone, not Taiwan or Mainland terminology.
- Cantonese phrasing may be used selectively where it feels authentic and remains readable; operational instructions should stay clear and standard.
- English does not control layout or information hierarchy.
- Official English branch names and addresses may appear as supporting operational information for maps, tourists and structured data.
- If an English version is commissioned later, it will be professionally adapted rather than translated word-for-word.
- Dish names retain recognisable Hong Kong terminology with short explanations where useful.
- All repeated operational information must remain consistent.
- The initial site declares `lang="zh-HK"`; a future English version would receive its own route, metadata and `hreflang` declarations.

### Traditional Chinese typography requirements

- Use a maximum of two Chinese type families: one expressive display face and one highly legible text/UI face.
- Recommended starting stack: Noto Serif TC or Source Han Serif TC for display; Noto Sans TC, PingFang TC or Microsoft JhengHei for text and controls.
- Final webfonts must be licensed, subset to the approved copy and delivered as WOFF2 where licensing permits.
- Do not place essential Chinese copy inside images or 3D textures.
- Author headline line breaks deliberately for desktop and phone; avoid punctuation stranded at the beginning of a line.
- Use Hong Kong punctuation, spacing, date, time, currency and address conventions consistently.
- Traditional vertical ingredient strips may inspire labels, but long explanations remain horizontal for readability.
- Alt text, form labels, errors, focus states and reduced-motion explanations are written in Traditional Chinese.
