## Decision

Use a **photo-first 2.5D composition**, following the method used for the Victoria Harbour project.

Do not attempt to make the bowl, noodles, toppings, broth or condiments as browser-built Three.js objects. The current low-poly scene is rejected. It is useful only as a rough placement sketch and must not be used as the visual foundation.

The finished section will combine:

1. One coherent photorealistic still-life image containing the wooden counter, finished bowl, spoon, chopsticks and four condiments.
2. The payoff lockup as real HTML over the left wood on desktop and above the photograph on mobile.
3. An optional desktop-only cinemagraph added only after the still composition is approved. Its scope is defined in `cinemagraph-plan.md`.

The photographic plate supplies realism. HTML supplies the readable payoff copy. The interactive order slip stays in 文記十大. The website does not need a second narrative video, scroll scrub or WebGL scene. The approved optional enhancement is a quiet five-second ambient loop with tightly masked steam; broth highlights are deferred until the steam proof passes.

## Why the current 3D approach failed

- Painted primitive geometry cannot describe noodles, daikon, squid, handmade dumplings or sauce convincingly.
- Food realism depends on irregular fibres, folds, translucency, moisture, oil, glaze and natural variation—not smooth tubes and rounded blocks.
- A believable bowl requires correct liquid depth, rim thickness, stains, ingredient occlusion and physically consistent lighting.
- The bottles require accurate silhouettes, labels, cap materials, contents and contact shadows.
- Building this as production-quality 3D would require detailed models, UV textures, material maps, lighting and art direction disproportionate to one homepage section.
- Separate CSS or 3D objects make the lighting and shadows inconsistent, causing objects to float.

The problem is the medium, not another missing shader. More Three.js iteration will consume time without reaching photographic food quality.

## Composition

### Camera

- Customer viewpoint at the counter.
- Approximately 35–40° downward angle—not a flat overhead view.
- Natural food-photography perspective, approximately a 45–55mm equivalent.
- The bowl is close enough to feel appetising but does not fill the stage.
- Condiments remain behind the bowl and do not compete with it.

### Placement

- **Left:** clean negative space reserved for the live HTML order slip. Do not generate another paper slip in the image.
- **Front centre/right:** white ceramic bowl with restrained blue rings.
- **Inside bowl:** dark glossy broth, thick yellow noodles, daikon, one modest serving of 玻璃辣魷魚 and exactly two 自制韭菜餃.
- **Spoon:** rests naturally in the bowl with its handle extending onto the counter.
- **Chopsticks:** a full pair resting naturally across or beside the bowl.
- **Upper right:** four condiment containers grouped as a secondary still-life element.
- **Background:** dark warm wooden counter with realistic grain and ordinary wear.

The condiments support the restaurant setting; they are not four product shots.

## Photographic foundation asset

Generate or photograph the entire bowl-and-condiment scene as **one image**, not as separate cutouts. This ensures one camera, one light source, coherent reflections and real contact shadows.

### Desktop foundation

- Landscape composition close to the final stage ratio.
- Minimum working resolution: 2048px wide.
- Keep sufficient clean wood on the left for the HTML slip.
- Protect the bowl and condiments from responsive cropping.

### Mobile foundation

Do not simply crop the desktop image if the bowl or condiments become cramped. After desktop approval, create a separate vertical crop or companion composition for mobile.

### Labels

- Do not ask an image model to produce readable Chinese bottle labels.
- Use simple unbranded cream labels or authentic label shapes without legible generated text.
- Do not copy commercial branding.
- If 麻油、椒粉、豉油、脆蒜 are required, provide them as accessible HTML outside the photograph.

## Reference roles

References must have explicit roles:

1. **Composition:** the rejected 3D image may explain approximate placement only.
2. **Bowl:** approved photographic references control bowl shape, broth darkness, noodle thickness and topping realism.
3. **Condiments:** shop photographs control bottle silhouettes, relative height, cap shape and grouping.
4. **Lighting:** warm directional restaurant lighting with deep but readable shadows.
5. **Materials:** ceramic, liquid, wood, plastic, glass and metal remain materially distinct.

No reference may introduce brand marks, extra dishes, street scenery, steam tables or people.

## Food realism requirements

### Bowl and broth

- Plain white ceramic with thin blue rings and natural glaze.
- Dark brown broth with depth, natural reflections and restrained irregular oil droplets.
- A visible inner rim and realistic liquid level.
- No flat dark disc, glass shader or mirror-like liquid.

### Noodles

- Thick yellow noodles with irregular curves and believable tangling.
- Moist but not plastic or uniformly glossy.
- Partially submerged with natural occlusion by broth and toppings.

### Toppings

- Daikon reads as cut root vegetable with slightly translucent cooked edges—not white capsules.
- 玻璃辣魷魚 reads as several irregular curved squid strips with warm amber-brown glaze and darker edges. It is a modest, partly submerged serving—not intestine, noodles, tentacles, worms or a repeated pile.
- The two 自制韭菜餃 have distinct handmade shapes, pale cooked wrappers, natural folds and restrained glimpses of muted-green chive filling. They are partly submerged—not identical floating wontons or fluorescent-green objects.

### Condiments

- Four distinct, physically plausible containers.
- Correct scale relative to the bowl.
- Natural contact with the counter.
- Restrained highlights and ordinary imperfections.
- Garlic jar contents look irregular and photographic, not repeated geometric pieces.

## Hard rejection conditions

Reject the foundation image if it contains:

- low-poly, CGI or miniature-diorama appearance;
- plastic noodles or capsule-shaped toppings;
- flat or mirror-like soup;
- malformed bowl geometry;
- floating objects or disconnected shadows;
- illegible AI text or invented branding;
- repeated food pieces or duplicated ingredients;
- honeycomb, tessellation, embossed networks, repeated squares or shared synthetic microtexture;
- excessive lacquer gloss;
- incorrect spoon or chopstick anatomy;
- extra plates, trays, hands, people or kitchen equipment;
- generated paper slips or duplicated order forms;
- steam that obscures the food.

## Motion policy

The still composition must work without animation.

The detailed production limits and checklist are in `cinemagraph-plan.md`.

Permitted after approval on desktop only:

- two or three restrained translucent steam wisps above the bowl;
- extremely small broth-highlight movement contained inside the bowl;
- no flying slip. The stamp change inside 文記十大 is a state change, not a flight.

Not permitted:

- rotating bowl or bottles;
- continuous camera movement;
- parallax or scroll-scrub movement;
- animated noodles, toppings or condiments;
- another narrative video or scroll scrub;
- motion used to hide weak image quality.
